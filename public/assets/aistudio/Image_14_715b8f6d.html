Targeted Lifestyle Medicine & Early Screening Framework

Cardiovascular Priority • Universal Screen: 30+ yrs ICD-11: BA00

Hypertension & Dietary Sodium Elimination

The standard Bangladeshi diet averages 9–11g of salt daily—more than double the WHO maximum threshold of <5g (<1 teaspoon). Over 72% of excess salt intake originates from added raw salt on steamed rice (কাঁচা লবণ) and high-brine fermented achar/chutneys.

Evidence-based culinary swaps: Lemon, ginger, roasted jeera & garlic preserve umami without sodium.

Bangladesh faces a unique "thin-fat phenotype" where metabolic resistance manifests at lower BMIs (22-24 kg/m²). High carb consumption of polished white Miniket or Nazirshail rice triggers steep post-prandial glucose spikes.

Carbohydrate Volumetric Swap

Reduce white rice from 2 cups to 1 cup; fill remaining volume with high-fiber local shaak (palong, lal shaak, pui shaak) and unmashed thick musur/mug daal.

Post-Prandial Movement

15 minutes of slow-to-moderate walking within 30 minutes of dinner blunts insulin spikes by up to 34% through non-insulin-mediated GLUT4 uptake.

Pulmonary & Environmental • AQI Defense ICD-11: CA22 / CA23

COPD, Asthma & Indoor Biomass Smoke Mitigation

Respiratory illness in Bangladesh spans two fronts: dense urban winter particulate pollution (PM2.5 >200 µg/m³ in Dhaka/Narayanganj) and rural domestic biomass cookstove (chulha) exposure impacting women and infants.

masks Urban Air Defense (Dhaka/Chittagong)

During Nov–Feb peak haze, shift outdoor jogging before 6:30 AM or indoors. Standard cloth masks fail to filter PM2.5; use certified N95 or KF94 during hazardous air advisories.

fireplace Rural Chulha Ventilation

High-roof cross-ventilation in kitchen outhouses reduces indoor particulate burden by 58%. Position infants outside primary flue draft during clay chulha fuel ignition.

warning Clinical Caution: Winter peak inversions correlate with a 38% rise in pediatric asthma admissions nationwide.
